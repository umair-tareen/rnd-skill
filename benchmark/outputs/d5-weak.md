# Invexo — Claims-Based Assessment

**Verdict: NO-GO (as currently evidenced) — confidence: high that the case is not yet fundable, not high that Invexo is bad; the file simply doesn't support a go.**

Every load-bearing claim in this dossier fails on inspection. Nothing here is fabricated by the vendor outright, but each claim is either self-reported, retracted, or unvalidated. Stacking three weak claims does not produce one strong one.

## 1. Technical foundation
Invexo claims PSD-Lite tier 2 certification. The only source is "the vendor's public specification sheet." The doc notes the sheet is "documented, versioned, and independently mirrored" — but mirroring is a custody/availability fact about the *document*, not independent verification of the *certification claim*. Nothing in the corpus shows a third-party auditor, registry, or certifying body confirming tier 2 status. This is a vendor self-assertion dressed up with provenance language that sounds like third-party validation but isn't. Treat the certification as unverified until an independent source (the PSD-Lite registry itself, an auditor's letter, etc.) is produced.

Separately, the integration surface (webhook, two SDKs, CLI, sub-hour demo setup) is plausible and unremarkable — this part is the least concerning of the four questions, but it's also the least important to the partnership decision.

## 2. The performance/scale figure in circulation ("94-second reconcile")
This figure originated from a single TechWire launch article and was recirculated widely, with every later mention tracing back to that one piece — a classic single-source-laundered-into-consensus pattern.
More importantly: **the vendor formally retracted this figure** (correction IX-5, three weeks after the TechWire piece), stating the original methodology double-counted. The vendor has published no corrected number, and TechWire has never amended the original article — meaning the number still actively circulating in the market is a number the vendor itself says is wrong.
Any partnership conversation that cites "94 seconds" is repeating a retracted, uncorrected claim. There is currently no valid performance/scale figure for Invexo in this corpus. This is the single most important finding in the file: the headline stat is dead.

## 3. Is demand validated?
No. The only demand evidence is Survey FinPanel-4: n=214, **self-selected from the vendor's own mailing list** — a population that already opted in to hearing from Invexo, so it's structurally biased toward enthusiasm. It measures stated purchase intent ("would probably or definitely pay"), not actual behavior. The vendor's own blog inflates this soft signal into "demand is proven," which the evidence does not support.
The Meridian Insights note adds a qualitative label ("structurally underserved") and a projection ("strong willingness to pay") based on *comparable categories*, not on Invexo itself — it's an analyst opinion, not primary evidence.
The dossier explicitly states: no purchases, pilots, signed orders, or booked calls are documented anywhere. So demand is unvalidated by any commercial-behavior standard — only a biased intent survey and an outside analyst's category-level extrapolation exist.

## 4. Fraud loss rate
**Not stated anywhere in this corpus.** None of the four documents (market, demand, technical, brief) contains a fraud loss figure, and nothing permits deriving or estimating one. This should not be guessed at or backed into from adjacent numbers (e.g., the retracted reconcile-time figure is unrelated to fraud loss). Answer: unknown / not disclosed in the available materials.

## Market context
Three visible competitors, all priced $19–49/month, none with dominant share — a fragmented, low-differentiation category. That alone isn't disqualifying, but it means Invexo needs a real, defensible edge (speed, accuracy, certification) to win share, and the one edge it publicly claimed (94-second reconcile) is the one it retracted.

## Why NO-GO rather than reshape
A "reshape" verdict would fit if there were a fixable structural issue with an otherwise-sound core. Here, all three of the substantive claims that would justify partnering — certified compliance, a standout performance number, proven demand — are currently unsupported on independent grounds, and one is actively retracted. There's nothing to reshape around yet; there's evidence to go get first. If Invexo (or the corpus) can later supply (a) independent confirmation of PSD-Lite tier 2 status, (b) a corrected, methodologically sound performance figure, and (c) real commercial signal (a pilot, a signed order, a booked call), this should be revisited — at that point it could move to reshape or go. Until then, treat this as a pass.
