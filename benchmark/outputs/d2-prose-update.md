# Orvana — Partnership Assessment (Updated)

## Verdict: RESHAPE (upgraded from NO-GO), moderate-high confidence — contingent on CAC disclosure

Two of the three findings that previously drove a NO-GO have been resolved by
new, independently-verified evidence: the retracted scale figure now has a
real, audited replacement, and demand now includes actual signed pilot
orders rather than only hypothetical survey intent. The third blocker — CAC
— is still completely absent from the corpus. That gap, plus the small size
of the pilot batch, is why this moves to "reshape and re-underwrite around
CAC," not all the way to GO.

---

## What changed in the last three weeks

A new document ("three weeks later") reports two substantive developments
and one cosmetic one:

1. **Independent audit published.** Meridian Assurance — a third party, not
   the vendor — completed an audit of the DAU figure and established a
   revised, verified number: **289k daily active**, with methodology and
   workpapers published. The vendor has adopted this figure in its current
   spec sheet (rev 5). This *supersedes* the OR-22 retraction: there is now
   a real, externally-verified current figure, not an absence of one.
2. **Signed pilot orders documented.** Order batch **OR-P9**: 9 countersigned
   pilot agreements, each with a purchase commitment and an executed
   signature page, verified by the same independent audit. This is binding,
   named-customer commitment — categorically different from the earlier
   survey's stated hypothetical intent.
3. **Cosmetic-only**: a redesigned pricing page and a new logo, with no
   changes to price points or terms. This is noise, not signal — flagged
   here only so it isn't mistaken for traction.

Nothing new appears on customer acquisition cost.

## 1. Technical foundation — unchanged

Orvana runs on the vendor's "HL-Sync wearable standard," per the vendor's
own public spec sheet. Integration surface is genuinely small — one
webhook, two SDKs, a CLI — and a reviewed demo took under an hour to set up.
This remains a vendor-sourced claim; "documented, versioned, and
independently mirrored" still means copies of the vendor's documentation
exist elsewhere, not third-party audit of the standard itself. Unlike the
DAU figure, nothing in the new document extends independent verification to
this claim.

**Read: still plausible, low-risk, and still technically unaudited — but no
longer the most important variable in the decision now that the two bigger
red flags have partially cleared.**

## 2. The performance/scale figure in circulation — resolved, materially

Previously: TechWire's 417k DAU figure had been retracted by the vendor
(OR-22) with no replacement, leaving current scale unknown, and the
uncorrected false number still circulating.

Now: Meridian Assurance, an independent auditor, has published a verified
figure of **289k daily active**, with methodology and workpapers available —
genuine third-party verification, not the vendor grading its own homework.
The vendor has adopted this lower, audited number into its current (rev 5)
spec sheet, which is itself a good sign — it did not simply re-assert the
original 417k or quietly move on.

**Read: current scale is now known and independently verified at 289k DAU —
30% below the original hyped figure, but real.** Any partnership case should
use 289k, sourced to the Meridian audit, not 417k, which remains a
disproven, retracted number regardless of its continued circulation in
secondary coverage (TechWire still hasn't amended its article).

## 3. Is demand validated, and by what evidence? — partially, now

Previously this was a hard no: only a biased hypothetical-intent survey
(Pulse-D3, n=214, self-selected from the vendor's own mailing list) and an
analyst's category-level speculation (Meridian Insights, not to be confused
with the auditor Meridian Assurance), with the corpus stating explicitly
that no purchases, pilots, or signed orders existed anywhere.

That has changed: **9 signed pilot orders (batch OR-P9)** are now
documented, each with a purchase commitment and an executed signature page,
and verified by the same independent audit that revised the DAU figure.
This is real, committed, transactional demand from named customers — the
exact category of evidence the prior assessment said was missing.

Caveats that still apply:
- The Pulse-D3 survey is exactly as weak as before — self-selected,
  hypothetical, and still not "proof" regardless of the vendor's "demand is
  proven" framing. Its status is unchanged by the new documents.
- Meridian Insights' analyst note is still speculation by category analogy,
  not Orvana-specific evidence.
- **n=9 pilots is a small batch.** It proves demand exists among some
  named, committed buyers; it does not establish demand at scale or a
  repeatable, low-friction sales motion. No deal size, contract value, or
  conversion-from-pilot rate is stated.

**Read: demand has moved from unvalidated to narrowly validated.** There is
now real signed commitment, but from a small number of accounts — treat
this as an early, credible signal, not proof of broad market pull.

## 4. Customer acquisition cost — still unknown

**Not stated anywhere, in any of the five documents, including the new
one.** No spend figures, funnel data, conversion rates, or per-customer
cost appear. The new pilot orders do not include what it cost to land them.
This is now the single largest remaining gap in the corpus, and it is
unchanged from three weeks ago: state plainly, CAC is unknown.

## Why this is RESHAPE, not GO or NO-GO

**Against NO-GO:** the two most serious problems from the prior assessment —
a retracted, unreplaced headline metric, and zero transactional demand
evidence — are both resolved by new, independently audited evidence. Holding
the NO-GO verdict unchanged would mean ignoring materially better evidence
that arrived exactly where the gaps were flagged.

**Against GO:** CAC is still completely absent, and a build-partnership
decision cannot be underwritten without unit economics — that has not
changed. Separately, the demand signal, while now real, rests on only 9
pilots; that supports "there is a real buyer," not "this scales at a
CAC we can afford." The competitive backdrop is also unchanged — three
competitors in a commoditized $19–49/month band with no dominant player —
so nothing here reduces market risk, only demand-signal and scale risk.

**What would flip this to GO**: a stated, defensible CAC (or enough
spend/funnel data to derive one) from the vendor. **What would flip this
back toward NO-GO**: if the OR-P9 pilots fail to convert to paid contracts,
or if per-pilot deal size turns out to be trivial. Absent CAC, treat this as
a partnership worth re-engaging with a specific, narrow ask — unit
economics — rather than one to walk away from or greenlight outright.
