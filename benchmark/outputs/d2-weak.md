# Orvana — Partnership Assessment

## Verdict: NO-GO (high confidence, based strictly on this corpus)

The one flagship traction number in circulation has been retracted by the
vendor itself and never replaced; demand evidence is a biased hypothetical
survey plus analyst speculation with zero real transactions; and CAC —
essential to underwriting a build-partnership — is absent entirely. The only
solid positive (a small, fast integration surface) is the least risky, least
differentiating part of the decision and does not offset the rest.

---

## 1. Technical foundation

Orvana runs on the vendor's "HL-Sync wearable standard," per the vendor's own
public spec sheet (rev 4). Integration surface is genuinely small — one
webhook, two SDKs, a CLI — and a reviewed demo took under an hour to set up.

Caveat: every technical claim here is vendor-sourced. "Documented, versioned,
and independently mirrored" means copies of the vendor's own documentation
exist elsewhere — it is not third-party verification or an independent audit
of the standard itself. Given the vendor's credibility is already damaged
elsewhere in this dossier (see #2), vendor-authored technical claims should
be treated as unverified until confirmed independently, not taken at face
value just because the integration looks clean in a controlled demo.

**Read: plausible, low-risk, but unaudited.**

## 2. The performance/scale figure in circulation

TechWire's launch coverage reported 417k daily active users, and that figure
has been widely recirculated since — but every recirculation traces back to
that single TechWire article.

Critically: three weeks after that coverage, the vendor issued a formal
correction (OR-22) **retracting** the 417k DAU figure, after an internal
audit found the original methodology double-counted users. The vendor has
published **no replacement figure**. TechWire has **not amended** its
original article, so the disproven number is still the one propagating
through the market.

**Read: the headline scale metric is known-false by the vendor's own
admission, and there is currently no reliable DAU figure at all.** Any
partnership case built on "417k DAU" is built on retracted data. Treat
current scale as unknown, not as "417k minus some error."

## 3. Is demand validated, and by what evidence?

No. The only demand evidence in the corpus is:

- **Pulse-D3 survey**: n=214, self-selected respondents recruited from the
  vendor's own mailing list (i.e., already-interested users, not a
  representative sample). 87% said they "would probably or definitely pay"
  $29/month — a hypothetical stated-intent question, not an observed
  purchase. The vendor's blog calls this "demand is proven," which is a
  significant overstatement of what a small, biased, hypothetical-intent
  survey can support.
- **Meridian Insights analyst note**: calls the segment "structurally
  underserved" and projects "strong willingness to pay" — explicitly based
  on comparison to "comparable categories," not on any Orvana-specific data.
  This is analogy/speculation, not evidence about Orvana.

The corpus states directly: **no purchases, pilots, signed orders, or booked
calls are documented anywhere.** There is zero transactional or committed
demand evidence — only self-reported hypothetical intent from a biased
sample, and third-party speculation by analogy.

**Read: demand is unvalidated.** "Demand is proven" is not supported by the
underlying evidence it cites.

## 4. Customer acquisition cost

**Not stated, and not derivable from this corpus.** No spend figures, funnel
data, conversion rates, or per-customer cost of any kind appear in any of
the four documents. This is a material gap on its own: a build-partnership
decision depends on unit economics, and none are available here. State
plainly: CAC is unknown.

---

## Why this adds up to no-go, not reshape

- Market: three competitors already crowding a $19–49/month band with no
  dominant share — a commoditized category, not clear whitespace.
- The only scale/traction signal is retracted and uncorrected — worse than
  having no number, because the false number is still circulating and would
  need active correction in any partner-facing material.
- Demand signal is biased-sample hypothetical intent dressed up as "proven,"
  with an analyst's category-level speculation layered on top — and an
  explicit admission in the corpus that zero real transactions exist.
- CAC, the number that would let you size the actual cost of the
  partnership, is simply not available.
- Easy integration is real but is the smallest part of the risk; it doesn't
  compensate for unknown current scale, unvalidated demand, and unknown
  acquisition economics.

**What would flip this**: a corrected, audited DAU/MAU figure from the
vendor; real transactional demand evidence (paid pilots, signed orders,
booked calls — not survey intent); and a stated, defensible CAC. Absent
those three, this is not a "reshape and revisit" — it's a pass until the
vendor produces them.
